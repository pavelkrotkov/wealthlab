# Tax rule mappings and validation

Research date: October 23, 2026. The annual tax adapter is being implemented in issue #4; the separate Annual tax UI and optional household tax plan are available. Household cash-flow conventions are in [ADR 0003](adr/0003-tax-cash-flow.md); itemized-deduction/payment coupling and final review remain in progress.

## Federal

Tax-Calculator 6.8.5 receives recognized annual income, not asset returns or gross sale proceeds. Taxable wages and employer-plan deferrals are separate inputs so regular payroll can use the appropriate wage base. Ordinary dividends include qualified dividends. IRA and pension amounts are their already-determined taxable portions. Passive income/loss inputs must already satisfy basis, at-risk and passive limitations; product modules will supply conditional allowed/deferred/denied treatments. The current adapter does not determine those eligibility rules or model self-employment/QBI.

Capital inputs preserve short- and long-term character and their respective carryforwards. Collectibles are a component of long-term results; the Schedule D 28% worksheet reduces that component for long-term carry and net short-term losses. Capital carry consumption uses taxable income before its zero floor, including the applicable standard/itemized and enhanced senior deduction. A household with insufficient taxable income can retain losses even when a nominal capital-loss deduction appears in AGI. The independent tests include both mixed-character offsets and low-income unused deductions. Authority: [2025 Schedule D instructions, capital-loss carryover and 28% worksheets](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sd). The existing local reference mirror was also checked by year and worksheet heading.

Do not infer household employee cost from Tax-Calculator's combined payroll amount. Regular employee wage payroll is half of `ptax_was` in the supported wage-only mapping; Additional Medicare is added separately. The package's `iitax` definition depends on its `soi_iitax` reporting switch. The adapter instead uses the invariant Form 1040 total `c09200`, subtracts refundable credits and Additional Medicare to report income tax, then adds employee payroll once. NIIT remains inside income tax and is shown separately as a diagnostic, never added a second time.

Independent 2025 joint-filer example: $500,000 wages, both adults 40, no dependents or itemized deductions. Taxable income $468,500; ordinary income tax $104,046; regular employee payroll $18,168.20; Additional Medicare $2,250; total household federal burden $124,464.20. The 12% bracket ends at $96,950. Authorities: [IRS 2025 brackets](https://www.irs.gov/filing/federal-income-tax-rates-and-brackets), [standard deduction](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501), [payroll rates](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751) and [Additional Medicare](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc560).

The installed package's default policy horizon ends in 2036. `package_law` refuses later years. The explicit alternatives hold 2025 law fixed or scale all its dollar parameters by a chosen index. Those alternatives are hypothetical regimes: even normally unindexed thresholds are indexed in the latter, and the frozen regime keeps 2025 provisions rather than implementing their later expiration. They must be labelled as assumptions in every result/export. No automatic long-horizon fallback is permitted.

## New Jersey work in progress

NJ amounts require their own basis and category ledgers. The [2025 NJ-1040 instructions](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/current/1040i.pdf), printed pages 11–12, require disposition gains/losses to net within that category. The [February 2026 GIT-9P](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/pubs/tgi-ee/git9p.pdf), pages 2–3, places a partnership's ordinary income, interest, dividends and gains/losses together in its partnership category. Do not copy federal carryforwards into NJ disposition income.

The NJ-BUS-2 alternative business calculation is a separate adjustment with its own business-loss carryover; the 2025 instructions at printed page 24 describe a potential 20-year carry. It must not be confused with a federal capital-loss carry or a deduction of disposition losses against wages. The calculation now has dated carry buckets, oldest-first consumption and the 20-year expiration boundary. The current four NJ business categories are netted separately before the 50% adjustment; disposition losses never enter this ledger. Authority: [2025 NJ-BUS-2](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/current/resbus2.pdf).

Retirement exclusions also need explicit person-level eligibility. In the 2025 instructions, printed pages 20–22, the pension exclusion and other retirement-income exclusion have different worksheet bases in the $100,001–$150,000 bands. Joint cases with only one eligible spouse cannot exclude the younger spouse's income. NJ gross-income filing thresholds use line 29 after exclusions. Medical deductions use a 2% NJ gross-income floor. Full-year, part-year, property-tax-credit/deduction and cross-jurisdiction-credit cases need separate validation before the NJ adapter is complete.

The 2025 full-year NJ core now includes basic/age/dependent and explicitly qualified additional exemptions, medical and listed qualified deductions, retirement exclusions, and Worksheet H property deduction versus refundable credit. Retirement-eligible income must be allocated to the qualifying owner; a younger spouse does not inherit eligibility. Special exclusion eligibility is separately asserted and can reduce remaining pension income. See [January 2026 GIT-1 & 2](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/pubs/tgi-ee/git1%262.pdf), pages 22–28. The older standalone GIT-1/GIT-2 bulletins are dated 2018 and were not used for current thresholds.

An exhaustive local cross-check covered all 2,000 $50 income bands in both 2025 NJ tax-table columns: 4,000 comparisons, zero mismatches. The $70,000–$80,000 joint/head/survivor table uses a $1,155 offset, while the printed rate schedule shows $1,154.50; applying the latter to the under-$100,000 table produces 100 one-dollar mismatches. The implementation preserves the published table values, with a regression at $70,100. Source PDF SHA-256: `fe7977419b70d0c3dcc392c328adc5f58f3b99937e0d2f79aecf6ac368872c31`.

This is still an income-tax core: other-state credits, refundable child/earned-income/care credits, use tax, insurance shared-responsibility payments and special assessments are not implemented here. The result identifies these exclusions; part-year returns and non-2025 law are rejected until explicitly supported by the surrounding policy layer. It is not a complete filing engine.

## Funding and payment conventions under development

The annual UI reconciles federal income-tax payments, employee payroll withholding, and NJ payments separately; entered payments never reduce taxable income. The withdrawal-funding solver evaluates the additional tax created by each proposed withdrawal, spends only available liquid resources, and reports unpaid taxes and spending separately. A refund is a receivable until its receipt date, not available cash. The solver returns a feasible funding amount rather than an optimal minimum, because tax cliffs can cause conservative over-withdrawal. Its iteration cap is visible and failure cannot become invented financing. The solver is now bound to explicitly permitted taxable-account sales in the monthly household UI. Remaining federal/NJ basis is reported, and unavailable accounts cannot fund spending.

## Filing-age conventions

The household template preserves separate federal and NJ filing ages. The [2025 IRS Form 1040 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/i1040gi--2025.pdf) and [Publication 501](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501) treat the day before the birthday as reaching age 65, including January 1 birthdays. The 2025 NJ instructions instead specify birth in 1960 or earlier for the senior exemption. An independent case for January 1, 1961 gives federal age 65 and NJ age 64 in 2025, with the corresponding standard deduction and NJ exemption. The local reference mirror was checked for both passages.

## State payment deductions and refund recoveries

The household ledger distinguishes actual NJ payments from accrued liability and preserves jurisdiction on unpaid balances. Opening and new NJ payments enter the federal deduction in actual-payments mode; the entered deduction is supplemental and excludes them. Entered mode keeps the total under user control. See [cash-flow conventions](adr/0003-tax-cash-flow.md) for opening timing, settlement and conditional refund semantics.

The [2025 Schedule A instructions, line 5a](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040sca.pdf) cover withholding, estimated payments and prior-year state taxes paid during the calendar year; expected/later refunds do not reduce that deduction. [Publication 525, Recoveries](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525) requires a tax-benefit analysis before treating a later refund as federal income. Verified October 23, 2026. The suite automatically recognizes no recovery when its prior federal deduction/tax/carry comparison shows no benefit, and otherwise requires a declared taxable fraction. It does not claim a full multi-recovery worksheet or infer an allowable alternative sales-tax deduction.

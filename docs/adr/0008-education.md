# ADR 0008: Savings-type 529 funding and higher-education allocation

Status: one education-account layer of open retirement ticket #6. This workbench does not close that ticket or calculate a complete household tax return.

The dated cash engine accepts savings-type 529 accounts under published 2025/2026 or explicit frozen-2026 assumptions, current contribution basis, net account-return assumptions and explicit program balance-limit schedules. Every relevant account for a program and beneficiary must be included, including accounts with different owners. A single NJBEST program identity prevents duplicate cap budgets. Limits are assumptions with effective dates; the synthetic example uses the provider's $305,000 opening cap and $525,000 September 8, 2026 cap. A program balance limit is not an annual contribution limit or gift-tax exclusion. Gift/estate reporting, prepaid-tuition contracts and Coverdell rules are not inferred from this model.

Only cash-funded requests increase assets/basis. A dated receipt and net return precede contributions, which precede withdrawals on a shared date. Requests are not retried automatically after later cash arrives. At a withdrawal the projection divides current value between contribution basis and earnings proportionally, with cent rounding. This is an explicit planning convention using projected transaction-date values, not reconstruction of a provider's actual Form 1099-Q. Supplied year-end tax forms must be reconciled before filing-level results. Remaining basis can exceed value after losses; full exit records unrecovered loss basis separately without claiming a tax deduction.

An owner payment enters household cash only when its recipient belongs to the supplied household cash pool. Beneficiary payments follow that beneficiary's membership. Direct-institution payments always leave modeled assets, and their tax recipient is the beneficiary. The output reports external disbursements so ending household cash cannot include a student's separate cash. The expense ledger records paid education but does not itself debit household cash: reimbursed expenses must already be reflected outside this workbench; an institution payment recorded here leaves assets once. Later household integration must coordinate these flows without duplicate spending.

Qualified higher-education eligibility is a supplied confirmation. The user must check institution, enrollment, eligible expense type and any room/board restriction. K-12, credentialing, loan repayment, ABLE/Roth/QTP rollovers, refunds and beneficiary changes require further eligibility models; the new federal K-12 limit is not silently applied to NJ or higher education.

Expenses must be paid in the modeled calendar year. Tax-free aid, credit-used expenses and other education-vehicle reservations reduce availability first. Explicit allocations reserve the remaining expenses across every account and actual tax recipient. An expense cannot be reused. If funding is smaller than the reservation, the unused reservation is visible and is not reassigned using future outcomes. Annual qualification can use a later same-year expense; it cannot fund an earlier cash request. No education credit itself is calculated here.

Each account and actual recipient retains its distributed principal and earnings. Owner and beneficiary labels collapse to one recipient when both identify the same person. The post-2014 elimination of QTP account aggregation does not permit duplicate expense use. Unqualified earnings, rather than gross distributions or returned principal, are ordinary federal income. Ten-percent additional tax is separate. An optional confirmed exception specifies the taxable earnings covered, bounded by actual taxable earnings; it does not validate disability, scholarship, military-academy or death eligibility. A scholarship's face value must not be entered as exempt earnings without the required allocation.

NJ higher-education earnings follow the supported qualified/nonqualified distinction. NJBEST principal previously deducted can also be income on a nonqualified distribution. Missing recapture stays unknown; an explicit amount is a conditional history input, bounded by returned nonqualified principal, not a fabricated FIFO/proportional deduction-history method. A supplied zero is an affirmative history assumption. The potential NJBEST contribution deduction uses only funded current-year household contributions, capped at $10,000 for one return with supplied final NJ gross income no greater than $200,000. Missing final gross income leaves that deduction unknown. The caller must include all modeled NJ items in this income assumption. Neither a deduction nor excluded earnings create a cash refund. Full deduction history, return/recipient allocation and annual NJ/federal tax integration remain unfinished.

## Sources and executed checks

[NJ College Affordability guidance](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/individuals/collegededuction.shtml), updated March 19, 2026, supplies the gross-income and contribution-deduction limits. [The NJBEST program description](https://www.ftinstitutional.com/forms-literature/download/529NJ-HNDBK), September 30, 2026 supplement, page 1, supplies the changed plan cap and effective date. Earlier unsupplemented text is not automatically current law.

The synthetic case funds $12,000 of a $15,000 request, then earns $4,200 on $42,000. It distributes $15,000 to the parent and $5,000 to the student's external cash. After aid/credit/other-vehicle reservations, $12,000 of annual expenses is allocated once. Parent/student federal taxable earnings are $1,536.80/$922.08; additional tax is $153.68/$92.21 absent a supplied exception. Parent NJ recapture remains unknown. Ending household cash $15,000 plus account value $26,200 plus external payments $5,000 equals opening capital $42,000 plus investment return $4,200. Checks cover shared dated caps, loss exits, actual recipients, funding chronology, expense reuse, partial funding, conditional exceptions and the runnable UI.

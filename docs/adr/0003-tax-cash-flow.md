# ADR 0003: Tax recognition, payments and household funding

Status: implemented; final validation and review in progress under #4.

A household can opt into an explicit tax plan. Every income schedule needs a federal and NJ character mapping. Each projected year has an annual filing/policy/deduction case. The first case can contain typed year-to-date income and payments already reflected in opening account balances; those amounts are never deposited again. With taxes enabled, the legacy untyped YTD summary must be cleared to avoid competing sources of tax context. Future opening income/payments must be zero; actual income comes from schedules and dated events, while capital/business carryforwards propagate from the preceding year.

Events separate cash received from recognized income. A noncash allocation can increase tax without increasing available cash. Federal and NJ basis and categories remain independent. The current household adapter supports one filing unit: two household members must file jointly. Separate returns require separate scenarios at this stage. The annual workbench supports its listed filing statuses independently.

Each month deposits scheduled income, receives previously scheduled refunds, pays outstanding prior-year tax from available cash, and recognizes this month's income/events. Optional permitted taxable-account sales then fund required debt, fees, spending and tax payments. Payroll withholding follows cumulative modeled employee liability; federal/NJ income-tax withholding uses explicit wage percentages. Dated estimated payments are additional cash requests. Contributions use surplus cash and do not trigger asset sales.

The explicit starting convention (`settlement_timing: year_end`) settles remaining tax in December, before other spending. Midyear funding covers withholding and dated estimated payments; it does not reserve cash for the entire accrued year-end balance. Accrued unpaid tax still reduces net wealth and may become a later funding shortfall. The optional `monthly_accrued` cash stress case instead settles each positive accrued jurisdiction balance every month, so a midyear funding sale also funds its accrued income tax immediately. This is an additional payment-timing assumption, not a claim about statutory monthly filing. Missed monthly settlements are flagged separately; their accrual stays on the balance sheet once rather than being added repeatedly to unpaid annual liabilities. Each jurisdiction's positive balance is payable separately; a refund from one cannot fund another's bill. Refunds are held as receivables until the following configured receipt month (April in the starter). Unpaid assessed tax remains a liability and can be paid from later cash. This is a planning convention, not a statutory filing/payment calendar; penalties, amended returns, and processing delays are not modeled. A missed scheduled payment sets a monthly funding-failure flag even if assessed tax is ultimately paid. Timing gaps are reported separately from outstanding liabilities to avoid counting the same tax twice in wealth.

Net wealth deducts accrued/unpaid tax and includes refund receivables, while liquid cash excludes unreceived refunds. The reserve diagnostic adds the year-to-date tax-liability run rate to annualized current spending. A partial final year reports tax on recognized income and entered annual deductions; it is not a forecast of a complete return with unknown later income.

Funding sources are opt-in taxable accounts with explicit federal/NJ opening basis, gain character, allowed/denied-loss treatment and a liquidity date. The interim adapter sells a proportional share of entered account basis in source order. It does not choose tax lots or assume borrowing. The solver includes additional tax payable under the selected payment schedule from its proposed sale; only a converged result changes cash and remaining basis. Tax cliffs can produce conservative over-withdrawal, so no minimum-sale optimality is claimed. Tax-lot selection and wash-sale machinery belong to #5. Intramonth payment sequencing is outside this monthly model.

The default `actual_payments` state-deduction mode adds opening NJ withholding/estimates, newly paid NJ withholding/estimates/settlement, and prior-year NJ balances actually paid this calendar year to the federal state-income-tax deduction. Entered `state_local_income_tax_paid` is supplemental and must exclude those NJ amounts. Opening NJ payments are assumed paid in the opening calendar year; if the timing differs, use `entered` mode and supply the total deduction explicitly. Other deductions remain annual entered assumptions. The alternative `entered` mode leaves the entire state-income-tax deduction under user control. Neither mode adds a second cash expense for a deduction.

Payments retain jurisdiction across years. NJ settlement precedes federal settlement, which is recomputed using the NJ amount actually paid; unpaid NJ tax is not deducted yet. The account-sale solver anticipates the additional state payment and its federal deduction. SALT caps, phaseouts, standard/itemized selection and AMT remain the federal engine's responsibility.

A refund does not reduce the current year's state-tax deduction. On receipt, its explicitly taxable portion enters federal state-refund income, separately from NJ income and cash. A standard-deduction year or a counterfactual with unchanged federal income tax and capital carry produces no modeled recovery. Otherwise `state_refund_taxable_fractions` requires an explicit 0–1 assumption by source tax year; without it, projection stops with an actionable error. This preserves conditional results instead of claiming a complete Publication 525 worksheet. The user must account for sales-tax alternatives, AMT, unused credits, allocation and other recoveries when establishing that fraction. Generalized multi-recovery tax-benefit worksheets are outside this core. NJ credits/assessments outside the published core remain explicitly excluded. No personalized household conclusion is inferred from the synthetic starter.

Profiling found repeated Tax-Calculator policy construction dominated federal evaluation (about 1.7 seconds per warm call on the development Mac). Reusing initialized policy/calculator state while replacing all read and calculated record arrays reduced warm calls to about 0.03 seconds. Fresh Records still performs package input validation, and one process lock protects reset/calculate/extract. Fresh-calculator equality across filing units/years and concurrent-call checks guard state isolation. Larger simulations will need batched records rather than assuming this scalar interface provides unlimited throughput.

# ADR 0075: New-cash option allocation units

Status: native helper for conditional issue #18 participation, 2026-10-25. Depends on [ADR 0073](0073-native-option-and-employer-exit-evidence.md); root-owned optimizer source-cash/ownership integration is separate.

## Decision

A new allocation cannot scale an existing stock lot or treat future appreciation as an already-owned asset. `OptionCase.opening_stock_purchase` supplies an explicit first-date integer share purchase and commission. It requires no pre-owned lots or settlement borrowing. The common native opening stage executes this buy through `OptionBook.fund` and the existing `Book.trade` purchase/basis engine before actual option premiums. Purchase cash and fees leave once; actual new lots acquire their current date and capitalize the commission into federal/NJ/economic basis. Unfunded purchase requests preserve account/reserve cash and create no shares. Dates, identities, integer quantities and money representability are validated before funding. The first-date dividend entitlement observation precedes this buy, so a buyer on an ex-date does not acquire the prior holder's entitlement.

Standalone and Household engines share this opening execution. Reports and terminal exit evidence export native stock trade history, so original buy, exercise/assignment and gross unwind quantities/prices/commissions remain auditable. The stress view also uses a fresh native opening book and actual acquired shares; a failed purchase cannot appear as invented stock protection. This does not alter stock/index market binding or create option quote history.

`option_allocation.scale_new_plan(unit_plan, count, per_unit_fees_confirmed=...)` accepts positive bounded integer fixed units only. All opening native capital is cash; there are no initial lots, separate outside reserve or settlement loan, and actual unwind is required. Contracts and new purchased integer shares, opening cash and absolute per-unit fees scale by the unit count. Strikes, per-share premiums/dividends, multipliers, dates, prices, market parameters and tax assumptions remain unchanged. Explicit per-unit fee confirmation is required; other fee schedules need another supplied profile. New long capital options derive mechanical short/long character from actual opening and supplied termination/expiry/unwind dates with the existing more-than-one-calendar-year helper; a legacy supplied character label cannot survive a fresh acquisition. The declared legal class and unaffected-holding-period assumption remain explicit. Position-specific nonzero tax deferrals and separately timed future openings/rolls cannot be rescaled into new units.

The helper validates complete conditional federal/NJ tax and actual-cash confirmations, and preflights actual premium execution order and QuantLib-based broker collateral using a fresh native book, including first-date dividend entitlement and supplied same-day exercise/close settlement. Actual negative settlement cash is rejected even if all contracts disappear. This establishes opening capacity only: future losses, cash settlement, collateral or delivery can still be infeasible, and actual native outcomes remain authoritative. `required_capital(plan)` returns cash needed in the new native account. The caller owns the real source-cash debit, account/market ownership, existing-plan exclusion and optimizer controls; this helper never moves Wallet cash or rewrites an existing household option plan. Root-owned optimizer integration must fund the exact returned account once and preserve original holdings/basis.

## Independent synthetic check

The new-cash covered-call example starts with $12,000 native cash and no stock. It buys 100 shares at $100 plus a $2 commission, receives $500 written-call premium and pays a $1 option fee, leaving $2,497 cash and $10,002 basis. A supplied February assignment sells 100 shares at $110 and pays $1 delivery commission. Actual closing cash is $13,496: $12,000 - $10,000 - $2 + $500 - $1 + $11,000 - $1. Both tax bases originate from the real new acquisition; the resulting $1,496 gain is short-term under its actual January/February dates, with no inherited legacy basis or holding period. No contract, shares, collateral or loan remain after the native gross exit.

Independent new-contract checks realize $698 from a supplied $12 close less a $5 opening premium and $2 total fees: the exact one-year anniversary is short-term, the following day is long-term, and an earlier supplied close remains short-term even if the horizon is later. These explicit close quotes are valid dated native scenarios; generated-market execution still rejects quote reuse without an adapter.

Two confirmed fixed units require $24,000, execute 200 shares/two contracts, create $20,004 basis and end with $26,992 actual cash. Household's separate $1,000 cash and $6,000 spending leave $8,496; shared annual tax inputs see the realized gain once. These are original hypothetical terms, not executable quotes or promised returns. The real Products form supports an editable new-cash example; the existing conditional warnings and actual cash/stock/basis/collateral output remain visible.

Shared realized-path checks replace the dated assignment marks with the same flat underlying batch for both unit counts. The written $110 call lapses at $100 stock, and one unit has only $3,497 early cash against $5,500 January-November spending, so $2,003 remains unpaid before its December stock sale. It ends with $9,499 actual cash and `funded = false`, despite a complete option-book exit. Two units fund all $6,000 spending and end with $19,992. These checks confirm that the new purchase does not reuse a favorable dated assignment or count unexecuted stock as an asset.

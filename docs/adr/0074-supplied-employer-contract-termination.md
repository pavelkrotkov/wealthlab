# ADR 0074: Supplied employer contract termination

Status: conditional native adapter, 2026-10-25. Depends on [ADR 0073](0073-native-option-and-employer-exit-evidence.md). Optimizer policy consumption remains a separate layer.

## Decision

An employer `termination` event is a noncash, explicitly sequenced declaration. It supplies a source reference and date, the contractual unit definition and remaining units, separately deferred value and cash due, and explicit claims that the contract is terminated, no future rights or contractual obligations remain, and all exit tax recognition has been supplied. Every substantive fact defaults to unknown. A source dated on or after the observation cutoff is unavailable. These are supplied assumptions; Wealthlab does not verify the document or determine an actual plan's legal classification.

The event never removes vested or unvested dollars, transfers cash, forgives liabilities, changes basis, or infers tax relief. Existing cash distributions, retention elections, forfeitures and supplied tax allocations retain their own dated operations. The observer requires zero native balances at the actual termination sequence and the current observation, reconciliation to the employer account, zero supplied remaining units/deferred value/cash due, and affirmative supplied confirmations. A later positive grant contradicts closure even if a later forfeiture or market loss returns its marked value to zero. Missing facts produce `contractual_exit_proven = null`; contradictory complete facts produce `false`. Only reconciled complete supplied facts produce `true`, labeled `conditional_supplied_exit` with `legal_verification_performed = false`.

Original allocation records now remain in `CompensationPlan.compiled_allocations` after compilation into TaxPlan. They must match exactly one corresponding zero-cash tax event, including federal and NJ inputs. Preparation remains immutable and idempotent, and serialization preserves the source audit. Their date, owner, identity and sequence validations remain active. This avoids treating a consumed economic-event list as proof that no later tax recognition exists. The public observer separately lists remaining supplied allocation events. Actual taxes and arrears remain in the ordinary TaxLedger; a contractual exit never certifies tax settlement or suppresses a later allocation. Unstated tax obligations remain unknown.

`compensation_exit_state.from_rows(rows)` remains the public consumer seam. Terminal evidence adds the supplied termination record, actual balances at its sequence, explicit reasons/status and remaining rights/obligation fields. Existing Household JSON controls accept these events; the employer result shows the conditional status, units, deferred value, cash due and unresolved reasons. No optimizer scope is broadened by this layer alone.

## Independent checks

The original synthetic fixture has $100,000 cash, $20,000 vested and $80,000 contingent employer value. A cash redemption and complete forfeiture precede the termination declaration. Over the supplied two-year illustration, $90,000 wages less $96,000 spending and $19,020 actual federal/NJ/payroll taxes leaves $94,980 cash and $194,980 wealth. A retain election leaves $20,000 in the employer account and $74,980 cash; it fails closure without inventing a payout. Earlier same-day termination, missing facts, retained rights and later grants fail or withhold closure independently of final marked dollars.

An additional $100,000 noncash allocation in the later year remains taxable after contractual closure: frozen 2025 joint federal tax is $7,743; the existing NJ under-$100,000 table uses the $98,025 midpoint and rounds tax to $2,641. Cash falls by $10,384 to $84,596 without a receipt. These are explicitly hypothetical frozen-law projection inputs, not predictions of future law. Mutation, repeat-preparation and JSON round-trip checks enforce exactly-once recognition; the native Household AppTest edits a required source fact and observes withheld closure with unchanged wealth.

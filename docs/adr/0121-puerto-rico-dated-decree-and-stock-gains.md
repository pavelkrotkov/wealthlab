# ADR 0121: Dated PR decree alternatives and plain-stock gain allocation

Status: accepted bounded implementation, 2026-10-25. Depends on ADR 0120; addresses issue #15. Monthly relocation remains issue #17.

## Decision

Add `PRComparison` around the annual PR case, a supplied dated decree and per-receipt marketable-stock gain facts. Keep the ordinary/denied-decree alternative runnable independently. The Annual tax / Puerto Rico incentives workbench edits complete original synthetic cases and exports the configuration, source slices, basis/FMV allocations and jurisdiction computations. Switching examples retains each edited configuration and result.

Future comparison years explicitly freeze the 2025 ordinary PR schedule while applying the enacted incentive regime; they do not predict later legal changes. Application date selects the statutory regime. A legacy application through December 31, 2026 can retain specified 0% interest/dividend and post-residence gain treatment before 2036. Applications starting January 1, 2027 use the specified 4% regime before 2056. No application implies a granted, effective, compliant, unrevoked decree. Actual effectiveness, expiry, residence and grant dates are separate facts. The old nonresidence-window claim (January 17, 2006 through January 17, 2012) and additional new six-year nonresidence-before-moving claim stay separate; six years alone does not establish eligibility. Legacy modifications/elections require actual amended terms and are guarded.

Unconfirmed or denied conditions prevent the incentive alternative, while ordinary computation remains available. Assumed residence, grant, eligibility, election and stock-classification facts keep the relevant output conditional. The app does not adjudicate those legal facts. New 4% treatment requires an explicit claim about other favorable entitlements and retains the more favorable computed ordinary alternative when applicable. Unknown compliance/property/grant costs remain unknown; a supplied annual compliance cost is displayed separately from tax, with no automatic deduction or economic-benefit claim.

The gain profile supports positive plain public-stock sales held more than one year, with supplied ordinary-stock character/source classification, active trading throughout and one continuous residence period. Sales must occur during explicit local PR residence and a qualifying federal full-year/move-in BFR profile; non-BFR/departure sales require a separate source profile. Controlled/pass-through entity interests, debt, inventory, US real-property/business connections, nonmarketable assets, mixed pre/post losses, other special character and February-29 holding/ten-year anniversaries need separate profiles. A mixed loss is never clipped to create exempt appreciation. Missing, duplicate or inconsistent receipt mappings are rejected.

Federal cost, local cost, first federal possession-tax-home FMV and local residence FMV are independent inputs. Proceeds and both gains must reconcile exactly. The federal source rule asks about any US non-BFR year in the ten preceding tax years; a proper FMV election can allocate post-possession appreciation to PR source. Without an election, applicable preexisting stock remains federally outside-PR source. Assets acquired after the possession start are not preexisting property. Ending that federal lookback can make the entire federal gain PR-source while leaving a locally taxable pre-residence component.

Local pre-residence appreciation remains outside-PR source under Code §1035.03(f), independently of decree qualification. Act60's 5% pre-residence rule instead requires recognition after ten elapsed years and before the applicable program sunset. The date-only supported profile uses a strict after-anniversary boundary. Local post-residence appreciation takes the separately qualified 0%/4% treatment; these special buckets replace ABT on those amounts. Ordinary 15%-eligible long-term gain remains in the general ABT base. Schedule A2 compares the supported 15% and all-ordinary alternatives before credits; personal exemption reduces ordinary income in the preferential calculation. Additional PR deductions and prior ABT carry consumption are explicitly guarded pending their separate allocation/history profile.

Regular and ABT foreign-credit limitations remain separate. Federal tax paid is reduced for locally exempt income before the PR limit. Source slices preserve economic pre/post amounts even when the federal no-election rule makes both portions US-source. US FTC with preferential/AMT/passive-basket adjustments remains unsupported. PR credit treatment of the Act60 4%/5% special tax on US-source income, eligibility of US NIIT for PR credit, mixed ordinary/preferential source-credit allocation and different-basis paid-tax reductions are withheld when material. No uncertain credit is fabricated from a general ratio.

# ADR 0013: Dated employment wage paths in the household ledger

Status: first layer of open employment ticket #7. Employer-linked grants, returns, vesting, forfeiture, distribution elections and joint employment/asset stresses remain unfinished.

An optional employment plan names the owner and existing salary/bonus income streams affected by each work path. The opening pay multiplier and dated pay-change, job-loss and return-to-work events are explicit inputs. Multipliers apply to baseline pay, not the preceding changed salary. Job loss requires an active job and sets the multiplier to zero; return requires no current work and positive pay. A pay change requires active work and positive pay. Event dates can arrive unsorted, but duplicate dates and inconsistent state transitions are rejected.

The adapter splits selected income schedules at decision dates and reuses the household's existing daily calendar-month accrual. It preserves each stream's growth clock, currency, owner and retirement cutoff. Redundant same-pay events remain in source inputs but do not fragment one cash flow into extra per-flow cent-rounding boundaries. A salary reduction does not automatically reduce an independently entered bonus unless that bonus is included in the path. Member retirement dates still end streams marked `stop_at_retirement`; a later return-to-work event cannot override that separate cutoff. Benefits, severance or legal compensation forfeiture are not inferred from losing a job.

An explicit annual tax plan is required. This layer accepts only streams mapped to federal and NJ wages and checks the worker's ownership against the source. Generated income receives the original tax character exactly once. Opening YTD wages/payments remain unchanged. Projection uses a copy with the employment plan cleared; the exported original retains its choices and produces the same result after reload. Duplicate path/change identifiers, shared target streams, unknown owners, unresolved assumptions and out-of-horizon changes block calculation.

Employment choices run before generated Social Security validation. A no-work path must reach the pre-FRA wage guard before claims are generated; changing household work assumptions cannot preserve obsolete wages or silently bypass that guard. A separate check starts with a wage case that correctly fails and confirms that a zero-work path allows its otherwise supported claim cash.

The household UI edits the dated policy and loads a complete synthetic employment example. January–June pay is $10,000 monthly, July–September half pay, and October onward zero after job loss; the member's January-2027 retirement cutoff remains effective. The resulting 2026 wages are $75,000. With the explicitly frozen-2025 tax cases, employee payroll is $5,737.50, federal income tax $4,743 and NJ tax $1,401, totaling $11,881.50. Starting cash of $100,000 plus wages, less $96,000 two-year spending and those taxes, ends at $67,118.50; the separate $100,000 retirement account yields $167,118.50 total wealth. Over-withholding is a receivable until the following-April refund, not additional income.

The NJ result uses the published $73,000–$73,050 taxable-income table band, not a continuous-rate shortcut. Checked October 24, 2026 against the local QMD 2025 NJ mirror and [NJ-1040 instructions](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/current/1040i.pdf), printed page 60, filing-status columns 2/4/5. Federal tax/payment conventions and existing independent checks are documented in [ADR 0003](0003-tax-cash-flow.md) and [tax coverage](../tax-rules.md). Future tax law is an assumption, not a current-rate guarantee.

Checks cover the complete cash/tax example, midmonth reduction, rehiring, selected bonuses, unchanged growth, invalid state/owner/tax inputs, duplicate records, JSON round trip, idempotent preparation, Social Security ordering and the runnable household UI. No actual household employment or compensation terms are inferred by the synthetic case. Later compensation models must retain independent cash, taxable allocation, economic value and forfeiture semantics rather than treating them as one event.

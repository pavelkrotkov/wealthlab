# ADR 0012: Generated Social Security in household cash and annual taxes

Status: first benefit-to-household integration layer under open retirement ticket #6. The ordinary and survivor claim workbench remains in [ADR 0010](0010-social-security.md) and [ADR 0011](0011-survivor-benefits.md). Medicare and wrapper workbenches are not extra household cash pools.

An optional `social_security` case lives in the saved household scenario. Before projection, a pure adapter converts the claim engine's following-month issued payments into dated USD income schedules, with zero additional growth and no household-retirement cutoff. Each generated stream receives one federal Social Security rule and no NJ income rule. The existing wallet, annual recognition, withholding, tax settlement, shortfall and reserve machinery then runs unchanged. There is no second opening balance, separate claim cash pool or gross-benefit deposit through tax events.

Generated schedules exist only in the projection copy. The exported original retains its claim inputs; materialization clears that field in the copy so it cannot generate the same receipts again. Record collisions fail normal scenario validation. Whole calendar months are required: the daily-proration engine must not prorate a whole Social Security check. The claim case must cover the household horizon, with matching member IDs and birth dates. Annual federal/NJ ages must match those dates, preserving the separate January-first tax-age conventions.

A tax plan is mandatory. Manual Social Security income rules/events cannot coexist with generated claims, and January opening tax facts cannot repeat generated benefits. Partial-year opening benefits remain explicit YTD facts already included in opening cash; generated income starts only within the projected months. Federal tax receives gross issued benefit income and uses the existing Tax-Calculator mapping to determine the taxable portion. Both amounts are now visible in annual tax output. NJ excludes Social Security and no payroll tax is imposed on it. No voluntary benefit withholding or Medicare offset is assumed; the annual plan's estimated payments and settlement schedule fund resulting liabilities from actual cash.

Until work-test deductions, later reduction-factor adjustments and the rest of household mortality accounting are integrated, this bridge conservatively rejects any positive annual wages for a member with pre-FRA entitlement and any member death before the household horizon ends. Wages include opening YTD, dated tax events and scheduled cash, even when earned before the claim. The guard includes pre-start entitlement paid inside the cash horizon, while retaining the entitlement year for work deductions. A prior December paid in January relies on the supplied no-prior-deductions history; new post-FRA January wages cannot be charged to that earlier year. Federal benefit taxation still uses the receipt year. A no-deduction checkbox cannot override changed household wages. This restriction is intentionally broader than the legal annual exemption or first-year monthly test; it is not a calculation of those tests. Post-FRA wages and pre-FRA claims without wages remain runnable. Separate-return federal benefit-tax treatment is excluded pending explicit lived-apart rules. Dated survivor cash remains available in the standalone workbench; joint filing, ownership and spending after death must be handled before attaching that result to a household.

The household form supplies editable claim JSON and a synthetic retired-household loader. A blank claim template uses unresolved PIA/eligibility provenance, so it cannot produce a personalized result until edited and validated. Household import/export retains all claim assumptions.

## Independent checks and sources

The synthetic household starts with $100,000 cash and $100,000 retirement assets, receives $152,250 claims plus $36,000 interest, and spends $144,000 over three years. With its supplied tax assumptions, ending cash is $144,250 and wealth $244,250. In a full $52,200 benefit year, joint interest of $12,000 yields $38,100 combined income and a $3,050 taxable benefit portion: half the $6,100 excess over the $32,000 base. Gross benefits remain $52,200, federal AGI is $15,050 and payroll is zero. Cash conservation, duplicate inputs, source immutability, JSON round trip, dates, changing wages and the runnable UI have checks.

Checked October 24, 2026 using local QMD tax-reference discovery plus original authority. [IRS Publication 915 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p915), Worksheet 1 and SSA-1099 explanations, distinguishes benefit income from the taxable portion and warns that premium/tax deductions do not reduce reportable benefits. The [2025 NJ-1040 instructions](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/current/1040i.pdf), printed page 8, exclude federal Social Security. The example explicitly freezes the tax engine's 2025 rules in future years. [POMS RS 02501.021](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0302501021) and [RS 02501.095](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0302501095) explain annual earnings tests and record-level charging; the conservative guard does not claim to implement them.

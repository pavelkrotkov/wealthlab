# ADR 0014: Conditional employer tranches and cash elections

Status: second layer of employment ticket #7. Uses supplied plan rights and tax recognition; does not determine the legal classification of a real employer arrangement.

Employer accounts now carry USD vested value owned by one household member. Their opening balances must reconcile to all corresponding tranches. Unvested value is contingent, reported separately, excluded from ordinary net wealth and unavailable for general spending. Every modeled employer account requires tranches; generic household cash contributions cannot bypass this book. Separate value slices can represent different vesting or liquidity dates.

Dated events distinguish grants, net returns, vesting, forfeiture, optional cash distributions and supplied tax allocations. Explicit same-day sequence numbers control order; input-list order does not. Net returns affect vested and unvested pockets separately, rounded to cents; embedded manager fees are already included, while external account fees use ordinary household cash. Vesting moves value without producing cash. A null vest/forfeit amount means the whole remaining slice after earlier returns or forfeiture. A fixed amount is capped by remaining value. Forfeiture does not infer a deductible tax loss.

An ordinary vested distribution requires the tranche's supplied liquidity date. Early vested tax cash and unvested tax cash each require distinct confirmed rights; unvested ordinary distributions are unavailable in this profile. A cash election reduces its specified pocket and deposits only the funded amount, exactly once through the existing household tax ledger. Retain leaves value in place. Unpaid optional requests are visible but are not themselves spending obligations; resulting inability to fund actual spending or taxes is still a household failure. The model does not infer borrowing rights from an account balance.

Tax recognition is explicitly supplied independently of those cash elections. The caller must confirm complete recognition and exclusion of these new in-horizon events from other annual inputs. Opening YTD remains intact. Allocations compile into the tax plan before earnings-sensitive Social Security validation; wages must match the tranche owner. Taxable allocations can therefore occur with no cash, including after a complete economic loss. Distributions have no additional inferred tax in this conditional profile: any applicable income must be supplied in allocation events. This does not establish tax-free distributions or sufficient tax basis. Partnership basis, section 409A/457A classification, vesting taxation, self-employment tax, QBI, recapture and hypothetical exit tax are not reconstructed. Missing legal terms require independent confirmation; the confirmations are assumptions, not legal verification.

The household cash engine budgets monthly. Economic events are date/sequence ordered inside that month, but results do not certify intramonth liquidity or estimated-tax deadline/safe-harbor compliance. Exposure is shown as a share of gross marked assets plus contingent value, distinct from after-tax exit wealth. The UI exposes vested, contingent and locked values, the value path and an event audit; JSON export retains original inputs, and preparation is immutable/idempotent.

## Reproducible synthetic cases

The baseline starts with $100,000 cash, $100,000 retirement assets and $20,000 vested employer value plus $80,000 contingent value. A $10,000 grant and 10% net growth create $111,000 total employer value. A supplied $20,000 taxable allocation is independent of a $20,000 unvested tax-cash election. Vesting changes the pocket split; a locked December request pays nothing. A later $10,000 vested distribution produces total cash receipts of $30,000 and leaves $91,000 vested employer assets.

Wages total $90,000; frozen-2025 federal/NJ assumptions produce $19,020 tax including employee payroll. After $96,000 two-year spending and receipt of refunds, cash is $104,980 and ordinary net wealth $295,980. Counting the original contingent $80,000 only in the economic reconciliation: $300,000 opening gross-plus-contingent value + $10,000 grant + $11,000 growth + $90,000 wages - $96,000 spending - $19,020 taxes = $295,980. Retaining the tax-cash election moves $20,000 from cash to employer exposure without changing taxes or total ending wealth in this baseline.

A named October-2026 stress combines job loss, a 50% employer-value decline and complete unvested forfeiture. It produces $75,000 wages, $15,245.50 taxes, $93,754.50 cash and $11,000 employer value: $204,754.50 total wealth. Taking the earlier $20,000 cash protects that amount from the assumed later forfeiture; retaining it ends at $184,754.50 with the same supplied tax allocation. This is an explicit dated what-if, not an estimated causal probability or a claim about an actual plan.

Checks cover conservation, cash/retain comparisons, reproducible stress, locked/contingent liquidity, complete loss with noncash income, confirmations, ownership, account reconciliation, invalid events, same-day ordering, round-trip/idempotence, the pre-FRA wage guard and both household UI examples.

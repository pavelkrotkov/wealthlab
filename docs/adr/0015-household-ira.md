# ADR 0015: Fixed IRA schedules in the household wallet

Status: another layer of open retirement ticket #6. Connects the existing ordinary-IRA engine to household cash, balances and annual federal/NJ taxes; it does not complete all retirement wrappers or choose a withdrawal policy.

An optional IRA plan explicitly classifies every traditional/Roth household account as an ordinary IRA. Owners and birth dates match the household in filing order. Complete owner-wide pools, separate federal/NJ basis, Roth contribution/conversion histories and exclusion from other tax inputs require confirmation. The bridge covers complete calendar years and USD accounts without embedded account fees, following the existing annual IRA engine. Workplace, inherited and annuity contracts are not inferred from a generic wrapper. Generic household contributions into these accounts are blocked until their contribution adapter exists.

Each year's fixed dated withdrawals, conversions and supplied net returns run through the existing IRA cash engine. On a date, returns precede orders; orders retain their explicit input-list order. Only funded withdrawals enter cash, once. A conversion moves assets between the same owner's traditional and Roth accounts and never becomes spending cash. Monthly balances follow the dated event journal. A later return or conversion cannot fund an earlier withdrawal. Unfilled optional orders stay visible; actual spending and tax shortfalls still determine household funding failure. The household does not automatically sell or withdraw an unscheduled IRA balance.

Ending federal/NJ traditional basis, Roth regular basis, NJ Roth basis, conversion buckets and the first Roth year become the following year's opening history. The existing engine retains RMD targets, conversion restrictions, early-distribution taxes and five-year clocks. RMD shortfalls remain separately visible; potential RMD excise taxes and first-year April deferral remain unsupported. [ADR 0006](0006-ira-withdrawals.md) describes the inherited engine's rules and source boundaries.

## Annual reconciliation and timing

Annual basis recovery depends on completed year-end pools. This fixed-schedule bridge introduces the reconciled federal IRA income, supported additional taxes and NJ owner-qualified pension amounts into the household's December calculation with zero additional cash. Actual distribution cash remains dated. It requires year-end settlement; monthly-accrued tax settlement is rejected. Estimated payments may be supplied separately. This is an annual reconciliation convention, not a claim that statutory recognition occurs only in December or that underpayment safe harbors are satisfied.

Until that reconciliation, the in-year household tax and net-wealth figures omit unfinalized IRA tax; the form and results state this limitation. Final complete-year results include it. No policy decision consumes a later annual basis result. Adaptive withdrawal selection, tax withholding from the IRA, contribution integration and intramonth liquidity are future layers; this bridge must not be treated as a complete retirement optimizer.

## Independent synthetic checks

The UI example begins with $100,000 cash and a $100,000 traditional IRA, plus an empty Roth. Two $20,000 withdrawals fund household cash, and a $10,000 conversion remains invested. Both owners are below 59½ with no supplied early-access exception. Frozen-2025 tax assumptions produce $2,420 in 2026 and $2,000 in 2027. After $96,000 spending, ending cash is $39,580, traditional IRA $50,000 and Roth $10,000: $200,000 - $96,000 - $4,420 = $99,580. The conversion is included in taxable income but not counted as another cash receipt.

The first year has $30,000 NJ pension income, $28,000 taxable after personal exemptions and $420 table tax. The second year's $20,000 gross income reaches, but does not exceed, the joint filing threshold, so modeled NJ tax is zero. Checked October 24, 2026 against the local QMD 2025 NJ mirror and the [2025 NJ-1040 instructions](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/current/1040i.pdf), printed pages 3, 31 and 57, including the $28,000–$28,050 band and filing-status columns. This example explicitly freezes 2025 law in future years.

Other checks cover basis differences and carryover, December returns changing annual recovery without creating earlier cash, a conversion blocked before the year's required withdrawal, a later Roth withdrawal retaining its conversion clock/NJ basis, rejected duplicate recognition, missing pools/context, serialization, source immutability and the runnable UI. Reducing cash demonstrates a spending failure despite remaining IRA assets. No personal balances, eligibility or legal exceptions are inferred.

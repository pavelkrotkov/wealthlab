# Compare complete supplied life-policy paths

A premium or stop-funding choice can change the insurer's future cash values, surrender values, death benefits and failure settlements. The optimizer therefore selects among complete independently supplied paths for the same configured policy. It never multiplies, interpolates or extrapolates an illustration to match a new premium amount.

Each `life_opportunities` record supplies a full native `LifeHolding`, provenance, explanation and explicit confirmation that the path's terms match its events. A `life_paths` choice maps an existing insurance account to one offered path. Separate dimensions can control different policy accounts; bounds are the finite offered choices and each dimension can be locked. Duplicate writes and unknown/wrong-account paths fail before market generation. Every offered path is validated, including inactive and locked alternatives.

Opening account capital, gross cash/surrender/death values, tax basis, policy debt, owner, issue history, ordinary/MEC status, exceptions and other tax facts remain fixed. Funding permissions and the household's independently supplied insurance need remain fixed. Allowed future differences are complete dated event/value schedules, stop-funding date, source metadata, matched failure exits, source withholding and payment-schedule completeness. Native validation requires full values and required-payment failure exits, explicit tax recognition, and payment coverage through the configured reserve horizon. A changed legal classification or replacement of an existing policy requires its own adapter.

The native household book pays actual premiums from the permitted cash resources, changes basis only after executed events and receives net-of-loan settlements once. An unfunded premium uses its supplied failure settlement, cancels later policy events, and still fails product safety. A surrender must satisfy the independent protection need and all existing complete-exit/tax-year/concentration conditions before ranking. A short example does not establish retirement safety.

The native Optimize UI adds an editable synthetic complete-path menu. Its three educational choices share $10,000 opening policy capital and compare zero, one or two $1,000 premiums. Supplied surrender value rises by $900 per paid premium; this is a synthetic illustration assumption, not a predicted insurer return. The independent flat-market identity is $310,000 starting capital less $48,000 living costs, $1 brokerage commission and $100 per premium: $261,999, $261,899 and $261,799. Actual returned policy basis is included once. The starter composes with fixed-product and cash-allocation examples while preserving default age-100, reserve and concentration checks.

The integrated suite passes 61 checks with 93.62% combined statement/branch coverage across the new path modules, compiler, validation and optimizer UI. Both new modules measure 100%. Independent checks also reconcile a supplied $200 taxable terminal gain, actual loan cash plus net-of-loan surrender, and a missed premium that closes the policy but still fails safety.

This layer compares complete funding/exit paths for existing configured policies. It does not infer a new policy offer, issue eligibility, future MEC conversion, term-insurance price or matching illustration from an arbitrary allocation weight. New-issue alternatives and the remaining product/cross-border integrations remain part of issue18.

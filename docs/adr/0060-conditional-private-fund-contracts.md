# ADR 0060: Conditional private-fund contracts in actual household cash

Status: bounded private-fund consumer for ticket #9; builds on [long/short economics](0033-long-short-economics.md), [actual sleeve cash](0034-household-long-short-cash.md) and [conditional supplied recognition](0035-conditional-sleeve-loss-recognition.md). No dependency added.

## Contract and economic accounting

An optional `HouseholdSleeve.private_fund_contract` changes the investor contract, while the existing long/short book continues to calculate actual underlying exposure, price P&L, yields, short borrow, financing, manager fees, turnover, maintenance and forced sales. This is a separate conditional fund wrapper, not a provider forecast. The investor explicitly assumes eligibility, limited liability and no calls. Internal negative NAV remains visible as fund insolvency and a product constraint; it cannot consume the household wallet or become household recourse debt. Ordinary margin sleeves retain full recourse.

An internal forced sale retains positive proceeds inside the fund, with no automatic investor distribution or legal liquidation. Requests carry forward through the supplied lock, calendar-month/quarter/year dealing windows, notice days and per-window gate. The gate caps net distributable NAV after actual exit costs. Complete-liquidation requests seek all remaining interests; they cannot bypass a gate or market lock. A zero gate suspends dealing. Optional partial requests are not spending bills.

Reserved proceeds leave the investment book once and become non-interest-bearing settlement receivables. They remain in account wealth while unavailable to household spending, collateral or tax funding. A payment is recognized at the first modeled month end on or after its settlement due date. Only actual settlement enters the household wallet, exactly once. Terminal pending interests or receivables block a requested complete exit; no fully liquidated after-tax ranking is claimed.

## Independent conditional tax book

This bounded profile requires confirmation of cash-only partnership distributions, no hot assets, no investor debt-share changes and a complete independently supplied federal/NJ outside-basis adjustment schedule. These confirmations are assumptions, not legal determinations. Cash contributions after opening, side pockets, incentive allocations, in-kind payments, section 751 recharacterization, mixed holding periods and investor liability changes require other profiles and are not inferred. Underlying loans do not establish the investor's tax debt allocation.

Basis is not marked NAV or the K-1 capital account. Dated signed basis adjustments run before that month's actual distributions, independently in each jurisdiction; an unsupported negative basis fails rather than being clamped. This schedule must include applicable income, used-loss, expense and other adjustments. The engine does not derive basis reductions from denied/deferred/allowed deduction choices. Basis/at-risk/passive/excess-business-loss eligibility and automatic K-1 generation remain supplied assumptions.

Actual nonfinal cash reduces basis; only cash exceeding remaining basis generates the conditional disposition gain. A confirmed completed cash-only liquidation consumes the residual basis, producing the independent federal/NJ gain or loss. Federal short/long capital character is explicit. Ordinary/passive versus capital allocation character remains independently supplied through the existing allocation model. Each jurisdiction's exit losses separately use denied, deferred or allowed timing; allowed/released losses require the existing after-limits eligibility assumption. Positive income survives denial. Every tax event has zero cash; ordinary annual capital/category limits, tax payments and shortfalls run in the authoritative household engine. Future deferred dates remain in the exit audit.

## Actual consumer and matched comparison

Products / **Conditional private fund** edits both complete household policies, source contracts, independent tax/basis inputs and market/binding JSON. One realized batch drives the actual household executor for both policies in deterministic, Monte Carlo, supplied historical or named-stress mode. Household capital/background must match; product fees and tax schedules remain independently editable. The reference has no shorts, at most 100% long exposure and 100% gross maintenance, with its own explicit cash-distribution tax proxy. It is not an ETF lot-tax model.

The default invented fund has December redemption proceeds settling after the supplied horizon, while its independent reference exits earlier. Outcomes show real bills, taxes, product/joint feasibility, unresolved exits and the difference in conditional wealth. Full source inputs, realized observations, exposure/cost rows, distributions, receivables and basis/recognition schedules export together. Imported private-fund households use a dedicated limited-liability/settlement display. Issuer inventory remains unknown, as in the underlying sleeve model. A two-year synthetic example does not establish retirement safety.

## Checks and primary evidence, October 24, 2026

Independent checks reconcile $100,000 fund capital plus $10,000 cash through partial and complete distributions; a $30,000 first distribution reduces $80,000 basis to $50,000, with a later $70,000 final payment producing $20,000 gain. Quarter-end gates, notice, lock and delayed settlement preserve wealth while blocking spending. A short squeeze can lose the full investment without taking the $10,000 wallet. A 1% gross-exposure exit cost reduces actual proceeds by $1,600. Separate tests show actual unfunded household bills/taxes, independent federal/NJ exit basis, denied/deferred/allowed losses, input guards, four path modes and real form submission/stale-result clearing.

Tax discovery used the existing local tax-reference MCP collection; no private taxpayer data or local reference paths are published. The 2025 K-1 instructions pp. 3–4 separate outside basis, capital accounts and loss limits; [current IRS K-1 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1065sk1) were checked. [IRS Publication 541 (December 2025), Partnership Distributions and Adjusted Basis](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p541) supports the bounded cash/basis computation and distinguishes complete-liquidation losses, hot assets and debt-related exceptions. Those exclusions require confirmation rather than assumed applicability.

[New Jersey GIT-9P (February 2026), pp. 23–28](https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/pdf/pubs/tgi-ee/git9p.pdf) requires separate NJ basis and distinguishes used losses; its dissolution examples put disposition gain/loss in the disposition category. The NJ basis schedule therefore stays independent of federal deductions. The [SEC investor guide on hedge funds](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/private-investment-funds/hedge-funds) describes eligibility and redemption restrictions. It does not validate the invented sponsor contract, gate, leverage or returns.

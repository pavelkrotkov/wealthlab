# Buy bounded new option units from existing household cash

The optimizer can compare zero or positive integer quantities of one fixed supplied option opportunity. This matches the native one-book execution boundary. Each opportunity supplies a complete OptionPlan, stock/index binding, owner, source cash account, bounds and explicit per-unit absolute-fee confirmation. Contracts, premiums, strikes, multipliers, tax assumptions, dates and pricing inputs stay fixed; native scaling changes only actual quantities, capital and confirmed per-unit fees. Newly acquired long-capital character follows its real calendar.

Zero units create no option account, book or binding. A positive choice debits its existing USD source cash once, creates a separately owned taxable account with exactly the required native capital, and lets the existing book actually buy shares, establish acquisition basis and execute contracts. Existing option plans/accounts cannot be replaced. The opening audit reports all new account capital separately from sleeve capital, along with actual source debits and unchanged total wealth. Combined sleeve/option purchases share the remaining source wallet, so an over-budget choice is explicitly unsupported.

Every offer validates before search, including a locked zero choice's inactive positive alternatives. Native preflight checks source confirmations, bounds, role-to-member/joint ownership, complete calendar/tax facts and every named market's factor/yield/lock conventions. One generated path per market checks these configured scope restrictions; every actual candidate path still runs the original native validation and economic execution. Known overlapping wash groups with linked ordinary/HSA books are rejected because no cross-book option wash adapter exists. Existing exchange/option ownership and wash guards remain authoritative. Initial capacity is only preflight: later collateral, settlement, cash, contractual exit and household spending failures still determine feasibility.

The composable synthetic starter offers cash, one and two $12,000 covered-call units. Quotes are invented supplied inputs, not available market quotes or generated tax alpha. It retains the native joint ownership. The actual unit purchases 100 shares for $10,000 plus a $2 commission, receives a supplied $500 premium less $1 contract fee, and eventually sells its flat $10,000 stock for $9,999. Thus its net gain is $496. From the same $300,000 opening wealth, $48,000 living costs and $1 original-stock commission, zero/one/two units finish at $251,999/$252,495/$252,991. Actual training and held-out cash agree, and native gross buy/sell trades close completely.

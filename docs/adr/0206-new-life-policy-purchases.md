# Select complete new life-policy purchases in household policy search

The optimizer selects a finite set of independently supplied new-issue offers using an atomic purchase-list dimension. An explicit empty list means no purchase; omission preserves no new issues. Existing contracts and independent owner-specific protection needs remain intact. Each owner must have an explicit supplied baseline need, including an explicitly chosen zero where appropriate.

Every offer validates before selection, including inactive offers. It must start with zero cash value, basis and policy debt, retain complete native premium/event/value/tax schedules and identify its source and matched new-issue assumptions. The engine neither scales an illustration nor extends supplied values. Actual premiums are paid through the existing household wallet and native life ledger; there is no opening-wealth credit or inferred tax saving. The existing protection, liquidity, reserve and actual terminal-exit constraints remain binding.

The UI provides an optional synthetic purchase starter alongside existing policy paths, options, exchange funds and sleeve allocations. Its two original $1,000 premiums and supplied $2,100 terminal surrender are educational assumptions, not an insurer quote. The generated terminal quote explicitly covers the configured reserve horizon; imported illustrations retain their original coverage. A $50,000 independent need makes both the no-issue and surrendered-policy examples unsafe rather than removing the need.

Checks independently reconcile $300,000 original cash to $251,999 without purchase and $252,099 after the supplied purchase path. A 24-candidate mixed-product search retains the same $340,000 initial capital and combines actual option-unit and insurance cash outcomes. Focused native/helper/control/UI checks passed 72 tests with 95.20% measured coverage; the four cash/composition checks passed again after splitting the test module to meet MI. All 1,465 first-party modules passed the unchanged complexity, maintainability and size gates, plus Ruff, formatting and type checks. Full combined-source CI and review remain required before release.

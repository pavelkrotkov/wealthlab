# ADR 0038: Replay an OWL retirement policy through actual household accounting

Status: independent reference layer of open retirement ticket #6. The prior cash-only engine check and limited benefit-stream comparisons remain; neither validates a complete withdrawal/tax/cash projection. This layer solves one representable case with the existing optional OWL dependency, then runs its gross IRA withdrawal policy through the actual monthly household engine. No dependency or tax algorithm is added.

## Matched inputs and explicit differences

The declared case has one living single retiree, age 65–100 at calendar year-end, a January 1 start in the actual current year, and two or more full years ending within the installed Tax-Calculator package-law horizon (2036). OWL's public Plan constructor chooses the current year and requires more than one year; the adapter does not monkeypatch its date or shift user dates. Cash, traditional IRA and optionally qualified Roth balances are supplied in dollars. Nominal investment returns, yields and inflation are zero, as are contributions, conversions, employment, Social Security, HSA, fees and Medicare. Federal IRA basis is zero. Independent NJ traditional basis must explicitly equal the opening principal, so zero earnings leave no NJ-taxable IRA amount. That unusual synthetic profile is not inferred for a real household. A positive Roth requires a supplied first year with its five-year clock completed; age is at least 65. Birth 1959 needs explicit conditional age-73 RMD selection to match the pinned reference; the native engine's broader conditional choice remains unchanged.

The adapter uses OWL's public balance/rate/allocation/spending/heirs-tax setters and `maxBequest`, with fixed annual spending, HiGHS, no conversions and no Medicare. Its solved annual gross traditional/Roth withdrawals become fixed January 1 native orders. Native spending is monthly, with any cent-rounding remainder paid in December, and native tax settles in December. OWL's cash withdrawals and redeposits are internal transfers: replay never deposits them as new household income. Unfilled requests, actual spending failures and native RMD shortfalls remain visible. A cent-rounded solver withdrawal is not silently increased to satisfy an RMD.

Native federal tax uses each actual year's installed `package_law`; OWL uses its own policy/forecast machinery, including its exposed assumed OBBBA replacement year (2032 in the pinned package). Later forecasts are not forced equal or represented as enacted future law. AGI and deductions are reported separately: OWL's combined deduction must not be compared to native standard deduction alone; the native audit now also exposes the already-calculated senior deduction. No underlying tax calculation changes.

The annual report compares marked nominal account balances, spending, tax, AGI and deductions. Its cash/tax residual is **native marked wealth − reference marked wealth + cumulative(native liability − reference liability)**. With identical funded spending and zero returns, a zero residual explains the wealth difference by tax differences. It is not a claim that tax forecasts agree or that either policy is optimal for the user's objective. Native shortages remain visible and can also disturb that reconciliation.

OWL's estate proxy is separately labelled. Its supplied flat heirs-tax rate is part of its source objective; it is not a native liquidation estimate. For example, a 30% terminal traditional-account proxy can induce accelerated withdrawals. This source policy is not the user's requested spending-safety-first optimizer, and it does not unlock unsupported household profiles.

## Executed synthetic checks

Pinned OWL 2026.10.10 and Tax-Calculator 6.8.5, checked October 24, 2026. The default three-year case begins with USD20,000 cash and USD200,000 traditional IRA, zero federal basis and explicit USD200,000 NJ basis, annual spending USD30,000 and a 30% source heirs-tax proxy. OWL gross IRA withdrawals are USD74,550 / USD50,900 / USD74,550. Both first-year income taxes are USD5,800. Reference later taxes are USD2,962 / USD5,800; native package forecasts produce USD2,890.92 / USD5,665.49. Ending marked wealth is USD115,438 versus USD115,643.59. The USD205.59 difference is wholly explained by cumulative tax differences; every year has a zero residual. Total native withdrawal cash is exactly USD200,000 and spending USD90,000; no source redeposit is counted again.

Other checks exercise a cash-only case, a qualified Roth case with actual native required-withdrawal targets, annual spending not divisible into twelve cent amounts, and unsupported dates, federal basis, NJ basis and Roth history. Broader OWL comparisons with income, benefits, conversions, returns, couples, partial state basis and other wrappers remain outside this validated profile. The [October 25 acceptance audit](../retirement-coverage.md) treats them as follow-on reference coverage, not as missing native functionality or universal validation.

Reference implementation: [official OWL repository](https://github.com/mdlacasse/Owl), pinned package `owlplanner/plan.py` and `tax_federal.py`. This is an executed software comparison; statutory coverage remains documented in the existing native IRA and tax ADRs. No private household data is used.

## Local UI

Retirement / **OWL household reference** accepts editable JSON or a local JSON upload. Imported/edited JSON must explicitly carry the `synthetic` boolean; a missing marker is rejected. The starter declares true, while personal inputs declare false and retain that marker in the compiled household and exports. Editing an invented scenario does not automatically turn it into a personal fact. It shows annual tax differences, a marked-wealth chart and separate native funding/RMD diagnostics, with expandable full annual and monthly audits. Exports include the declared inputs and complete reference, compiled native scenario and actual results. Changing input sources or submitting invalid inputs clears an older result. The reference requires the existing optional `benchmarks` extra; without it, the UI explains how to enable the comparison.

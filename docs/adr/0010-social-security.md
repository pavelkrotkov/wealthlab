# ADR 0010: Ordinary Social Security claims and a limited Owl reference

Status: own/ordinary-spouse claim layer of open retirement ticket #6. Survivor/work-test behavior, full benefit histories and household integration remain unfinished.

The initial claim model supports births after January 1, 1943, using the corresponding eight-percent annual delayed-credit rate. It accepts a supplied January-2026 PIA as an SSA dime amount, insured-worker status, ordinary claim month and reciprocal partner identity. Eligibility is supplied and checked for basic consistency; the engine does not infer insured or marital eligibility from a household relationship. An uninsured spouse can have zero own PIA and receive a confirmed benefit on an insured partner's record.

Current claim rules are explicitly held fixed into the future. The case confirms survival of all members and no earnings-test deductions, other record auxiliaries, prior deductions, suspensions, disability or restricted-claim history. These are conditional scope requirements, not automatic findings. Unsupported histories block or remain outside this layer; ticket #6 is not complete. Changing future wages requires revalidating the no-deduction assumption before this result can enter the household engine or optimizer. No benefit from an omitted child or survivor is silently claimed as modeled.

Owl's stable public `getFRAs` supplies the pinned retirement FRA schedule. The adapter converts it to an actual attainment month: a first-day birthday can move that month backward, and January-first changes the birth cohort. The own/spouse age-62 throughout-month rule is separate: birthdays on day 1 or 2 can start that birthday month, others the next month. Inputs must identify the first of an eligible claim month. Claim dates remain fixed choices on this screen.

Own early reductions use exact rational monthly rates, with dime truncation at the benefit boundary. Reducing a monetary amount and then truncating the result avoids floating-point errors on exact dime boundaries. Delayed credits stop at age 70. Before 70, credits earned in the claim year do not enter initial benefits until the following January entitlement (paid in February). At 70 they can enter without waiting until the next January. Claims after 70 remain valid native scenarios but earn no additional credits.

An ordinary spouse add-on begins no earlier than both claim dates. It is based on half the partner's PIA, not their delayed benefit. Own and excess-spouse amounts are reduced separately for their own starting dates. The recipient's own delayed credits reduce the auxiliary amount under SSA dual-entitlement rules. A high delayed own benefit can absorb the entire add-on; adding a full spouse excess on top would overstate benefits.

PIA is normalized to January 2026, including the December-2025 COLA. Future assumed COLAs apply to December entitlement and therefore following-January cash. Each year's PIA truncates to a dime. Entitlement before offsets remains separate from issued no-offset cash, which truncates to whole dollars. No Medicare premium or tax offset is applied in this layer. Their eventual integration must preserve SSA's offset/rounding order and tax reporting. Payment month follows entitlement month; actual Wednesday payment dates are not predicted. Prior December is included for January cash, while final December entitlement is not counted as issued until the next year.

## Executed reuse comparisons

The integer-PIA, zero-COLA own-benefit cases at 62/FRA/70 match both independent SSA values and pinned Owl annual amounts: $8,400/$12,000/$14,880 for a $1,000 PIA and January 2, 1960 birth. No host-current-year default is used.

The combined Owl stream cannot be authoritative for every case. With same-age spouses, PIAs $2,500/$1,000, January 2, 1960 births and age-70 January-2030 claims, native full-year 2031 lower-recipient benefits are $15,000. Their own benefit is $1,240/month plus $10 auxiliary, reaching the $1,250 full spouse amount. Owl 2026.10.10 instead returns $17,880: it adds a $250 spouse excess without the required $240 DRC offset. The reproduced $2,880 annual difference is visible in the UI and tested; it never replaces native cash.

Owl also clips a spouse payment start to the first plan year while retaining the original partial first-year fraction, and its simple own factor does not reproduce every pre-70 claim-year credit recognition or SSA rounding case. The limited reference labels those differences. Nonzero COLA, fractional-dollar PIA, eligibility differences, claims after 70 and PIA beyond Owl's signed-32-bit range are explicitly excluded rather than silently normalized or allowed to invalidate a native result. Reference display values use cents to remove irrelevant floating-point noise. They remain a numerical comparison, not verified SSA entitlement.

The official [AnyPIA source](https://www.ssa.gov/OACT/anypia/source.html) and [batch interface](https://www.ssa.gov/OACT/anypia/anypiab.html) were researched for future earnings-history/PIA checks. They offer a comprehensive C++ engine, with a compiler/Boost integration cost; no source download, new dependency or adoption was performed in this layer. Native source-backed monthly arithmetic remains small, and existing Owl FRA data and supported reference cases are reused.

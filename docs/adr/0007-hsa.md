# ADR 0007: HSA cash funding and medical receipts

Status: HSA cash layer of #6; education and household integration remain unfinished.

October 24 update: [explicit frozen-2026 forward contribution assumptions](0016-future-contribution-assumptions.md) now extend the cash workbench to later calendar years. Actual-year ages, dates and monthly eligibility remain separate from the frozen dollar table.


The HSA workbench uses explicit 2025/2026 monthly eligibility forecasts and cash-only account balances. Coverage is supplied for each first-of-month date, with Medicare, disqualifying other coverage and claimable-dependent flags. Turning 65 does not automatically enroll a person in Medicare. Married eligible spouses share a family base if either has family coverage; the default split is equal, and explicit shares cannot exceed the shared amount. Each age-55 catch-up belongs to its own eligible owner. If one spouse becomes ineligible, the eligible spouse can use the whole family base. Monthly fractions are accumulated before conservative downward cent rounding.

Only the monthly method is implemented: last-month-rule benefits and testing-period recapture remain unfinished. Contribution dates can follow the end of coverage while using earned annual room. The user confirms all current-year contribution requests and opening history. Existing excess contributions, prior-year deposits after year end, qualified IRA-to-HSA funding and rollover corrections need separate models.

Personal requests draw available cash; direct employer requests add external funding. Both consume the owner's common limit across all configured HSAs, but only funded amounts use room. Limit-blocked amounts and cash shortfalls remain separate. Bank interest is a dated cash event. Personal transfers and withdrawals conserve total capital; employer contributions, external cash receipts and interest are the only additions. Salary reduction and investment securities are outside this layer.

Medical receipts describe expenses already paid outside this workbench. Their cash payment must already be reflected in the relevant household history. The user confirms qualified medical status and eligible family relationships. Amounts reimbursed elsewhere or deducted federally are unavailable for a later tax-free reimbursement. Expenses predating the withdrawing owner's HSA establishment or not yet paid cannot supply qualified capacity. One global remaining-receipt ledger prevents reuse across multiple accounts or spouses.

A `qualified_only` request pays no more than both available HSA cash and eligible receipts. An `allow_nonqualified` request can withdraw other cash, with ordinary federal income and separately calculated twenty-percent additional tax. Age 65 or a supplied disability date removes the additional tax without removing ordinary income. Death/beneficiary treatment is unfinished. These are tax ingredients, not an annual tax saving or cash tax payment. Household spending and annual filing-engine integration remain required.

NJ treatment defaults to unconfirmed and remains unknown. The optional taxable-bank-interest mode explicitly assumes no personal HSA deduction, taxable employer contributions and current taxable bank interest; it does not apply federal HSA exemption to NJ. These ingredients require payroll reconciliation before household tax integration, so an employer amount already included in NJ wages cannot be added twice. This conditional cash mode does not establish legal treatment of investment transactions, previously held securities or special interest exemptions.

## Sources and checks

[IRS Publication 969 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), checked October 24, 2026, supplies monthly eligibility, spouse/catch-up sharing, Medicare, establishment-date and receipt restrictions, contribution timing and nonqualified-distribution distinctions. [Revenue Procedure 2025-19](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-19.pdf) supplies published 2026 limits. Existing QMD Form 8889 instructions (2024) were used for discovery only; their earlier dollar limits were not substituted. [Fidelity's September 10, 2026 HSA guide](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/smart-money/hsa-rollover) corroborates separate NJ investment-income treatment; it is provider guidance, not a substitute for the still-required NJ authority check. No NJ bill was treated as enacted law.

The synthetic spouses have $7,562.50/$2,687.50 of 2026 room as one begins Medicare in July. Requests fund only $2,000 of personal contributions with available cash. A $3,000 medical receipt supplies $1,500 after prior claims. The second spouse cannot reuse it. Their after-65 nonqualified withdrawal remains income while avoiding the additional tax; the younger owner's pre-establishment expense supplies no qualified reimbursement. Initial cash/assets plus employer funding and interest reconcile to ending cash/assets.

HSA investment/NJ lot accounting, all-year rule assumptions, last-month/testing-period cases, death/rollovers/excess correction, full medical eligibility, annual tax mapping and actual household expense funding remain part of the open retirement/integration scope.
